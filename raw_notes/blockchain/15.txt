## **1.2 Definition of a Bitcoin wallet**

**A Bitcoin wallet is a software application or physical device used to generate, store and manage cryptographic keys, allowing users to send, receive and manage Bitcoin transactions.**

Your teacher's PPT specifically lists these wallet functions:

2. Storing cryptographic keys.
3. Sending and receiving Bitcoin.
4. Backing up keys.
5. Keeping track of the available balance.

In simple language, a Bitcoin wallet is a tool that helps you manage your Bitcoin ownership and transactions.

## **1.3 Does a Bitcoin wallet actually store bitcoins?**

Very important concept

No. A Bitcoin wallet does not store actual bitcoins.

Let's understand this with an example.

Suppose Aman has 2 BTC in his wallet.

Where are those 2 BTC?

They are represented by unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs) recorded on the Bitcoin blockchain.

The wallet stores the keys needed to satisfy the conditions for spending those outputs.

The wallet also examines blockchain information to calculate the balance associated with the keys it manages.

### **Real-life example**

Imagine that your bank stores your money in its banking system.

Your ATM card does not contain the actual money. It gives you a way to access and use the money associated with your bank account.

Similarly:

- Bitcoin blockchain = records the transaction outputs.
- Bitcoin wallet = manages the keys used to control those outputs.
- Private key = gives the ability to authorise spending under the relevant spending conditions.

**Important difference:** Bitcoin is not stored inside the wallet. The wallet stores the keys that provide control over Bitcoin recorded on the blockchain.

- Transaction information
- Wallet-related metadata

The most important item is the **private key**.

Why? Because the private key is used to create digital signatures authorising transactions.

# **Topic 2: Understanding Private Keys, Public Keys and Bitcoin Addresses**

Before learning wallet types, you must understand these three terms.

They are the foundation of Bitcoin wallets.

## **2.1 What is a private key?**

A **private key is a secret cryptographic number used to authorise the spending of Bitcoin.**

In Bitcoin, private keys are 256-bit numbers generated within the valid range specified by the secp256k1 elliptic curve.

Your teacher's PPT explains that private keys are randomly generated and are commonly represented in Wallet Import Format (WIF) for importing or exporting keys.

### **Simple example**

Imagine that Aman owns Bitcoin.

His wallet has a private key.

When Aman wants to send Bitcoin to Rahul, the wallet uses the private key to digitally sign the transaction.

The private key proves that Aman can authorise the spending of the relevant Bitcoin.

**Important:** Never share your private key with another person.

Anyone who obtains a private key controlling your Bitcoin may be able to spend the associated funds.

## **2.2 What is a public key?**

A **public key is a cryptographic key derived from a private key and used by others to verify digital signatures.**

In Bitcoin, public keys are generated using Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC), specifically the secp256k1 curve.

The public key is not secret like the private key.

### **Example**

Suppose Aman has:

- Private key: Secret
- Public key: Derived from the private key

When Aman signs a Bitcoin transaction, other nodes can use the corresponding public key to verify that the signature is valid.

This helps establish that the transaction was authorised by someone controlling the corresponding private key.

## **2.3 What is a Bitcoin address?**

A **Bitcoin address is an identifier that a sender can use to specify where Bitcoin should be sent.**

Think of it like a bank account number.

If Rahul wants to receive Bitcoin from Aman, Rahul shares his Bitcoin address with Aman.

Aman enters that address in his wallet to create the payment.

Example of a traditional Bitcoin address:

EXAMPLE BITCOIN ADDRESS

## **1ANAguGG8bikEv2fYsTBnRUmx7QUcK58wt**

Example from your teacher's Bitcoin material.

A Bitcoin address is not the private key. It is an identifier used in the transaction's spending conditions.

Let's understand their relationship.

## **2.5 How is a traditional Bitcoin address generated?**

Your teacher's *All topics of bitcoin.pptx* explains the traditional public-key-hash address-generation process.

Let's understand each step.

**Step 1:** Generate a private key.

**Step 2:** Derive the public key from the private key.

**Step 3:** Apply SHA-256 to the public key.

**Step 4:** Apply RIPEMD-160 to the SHA-256 result.

**Step 5:** Add the relevant version information.

**Step 6:** Calculate a checksum using double SHA-256.

**Step 7:** Encode the result using Base58Check.

The result is a traditional Bitcoin address such as a P2PKH address.

### **What is Base58Check?**

Base58Check is an encoding method used to represent Bitcoin data in a human-readable format, with a checksum to help detect typing errors.

Your teacher's PPT mentions that traditional addresses can begin with:

- `1` — commonly associated with P2PKH addresses.
- `3` — commonly associated with P2SH addresses.

Modern Bitcoin also supports SegWit addresses beginning with `bc1`, which we'll encounter in the payments and advanced-protocol modules.

**Exam point:** Do not write that every Bitcoin address begins with 1 or 3. That applies to the traditional address types covered in the older public-key-hash discussion.

# **Topic 3: How Does a Bitcoin Wallet Work?**

Now let's understand how a wallet is used in real life.

Suppose Aman wants to send 0.5 BTC to Rahul.

## **3.1 Receiving Bitcoin**

Rahul wants to receive Bitcoin.

1. Rahul opens his Bitcoin wallet.
2. The wallet provides a Bitcoin receiving address.
3. Rahul shares the address with Aman.
4. Aman creates a transaction using Rahul's address.
5. The transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network.
6. Once included in an accepted block, the payment is recorded on the blockchain.
7. Rahul's wallet detects the relevant transaction and updates its displayed balance.

**Rahul opens wallet**

**Wallet generates or displays receiving address**

**Rahul shares address with Aman**

**Aman sends Bitcoin**

**Transaction is confirmed and Rahul's wallet recognises the payment**

## **3.2 Sending Bitcoin**

Now suppose Aman wants to send 0.5 BTC to Rahul.

The process is:

1. Aman opens his wallet.
2. He enters Rahul's Bitcoin address.
3. He enters the amount, 0.5 BTC.
4. The wallet selects suitable unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs).
5. The wallet creates a transaction with inputs and outputs.
6. The wallet signs the transaction using the required private key.
7. The transaction is broadcast to Bitcoin nodes.
8. Miners may include it in a block.
9. The transaction receives confirmations as additional blocks build on that block.

**Important:** A wallet does not send a private key to Rahul or to the Bitcoin network. It uses the key locally to produce the required signature.

## **3.3 What is a UTXO?**

UTXO stands for **Unspent Transaction Output**.

Bitcoin uses the UTXO model instead of maintaining a simple account balance in the way many traditional banking systems do.

Let's understand with an example.

Suppose Aman receives:

- 0.4 BTC from Rahul.
- 0.8 BTC from Priya.

Aman now controls UTXOs totalling 1.2 BTC.

He wants to send 0.5 BTC to someone.

His wallet might construct a transaction like this:

**Transaction example**

**Inputs**

# **1.2 BTC**

0.4 + 0.8 BTC

**Outputs**

# **1.198 BTC**

0.5 BTC payment + 0.698 BTC change

**Transaction fee**

## **0.002 BTC**

Here:

\\[ \text{Inputs}=\text{Outputs}+\text{Fee} \\]

\\[ 1.2=0.5+0.698+0.002 \\]

Aman sends 0.5 BTC to the recipient, receives 0.698 BTC as change, and pays 0.002 BTC as the transaction fee.

The original UTXOs are spent, and new UTXOs are created.

**Remember:** the wallet calculates the balance by examining the UTXOs associated with the keys or scripts it manages.

## **4.1 Non-deterministic wallet (JBOK wallet)**

JBOK = Just a Bunch of Keys

### **Definition**

A **non-deterministic wallet is a wallet in which multiple private keys are generated independently and randomly rather than being derived from one common seed.**

Your teacher's PPT explains that Bitcoin Core historically generated a number of keys when first started and generated additional keys as required.

### **How does it work?**

Suppose Aman creates a non-deterministic wallet.

The wallet independently generates several random private keys:

Randomly generated

Private key 3

Randomly generated

Private key 4

Randomly generated

Private key 5

Randomly generated

Randomly generated

Each private key is generated independently.

The keys are not generated from one common seed.

### **Advantages**

1. Private keys are generated randomly.
2. Each key is independent.
3. Straightforward key-generation concept.

### **Disadvantages**

1. Managing many independent keys is difficult.
2. Each key needs to be backed up appropriately.
3. Losing a private key can mean losing control of the Bitcoin associated with it.
4. Repeated backup management can become complicated.

### **Real-life example**

Imagine you have ten lockers, and each locker has a completely different key.

If you lose the key to one locker, you cannot simply use another locker's key.

This is similar to a non-deterministic wallet.

## **4.2 Deterministic wallet**

### **Definition**

A **deterministic wallet is a wallet in which multiple private keys are derived from a single randomly generated seed using cryptographic functions.**

Your teacher's PPT describes keys being derived from a random seed through hash functions.

### **How does it work?**

Suppose Aman creates a deterministic wallet.

The wallet generates one random seed.

Using this seed, it derives multiple private keys.

**Random seed**

One starting value

Cryptographic derivation

Private key 1

Private key 2

Private key 3

The same seed and derivation procedure can regenerate the same keys.

### **Advantages**

1. Only one seed needs to be backed up.
2. Key recovery is easier.
3. Key management is simpler than independently backing up many keys.
4. The wallet can regenerate its keys from the seed.

### **Disadvantages**

1. Losing the seed can mean losing access to all keys derived from it.
2. Anyone obtaining the seed may be able to recover the wallet's keys.
3. The seed must be stored securely.

### **Real-life example**

Imagine you have one master key that can generate the keys for ten different lockers.

If you safely preserve the master key, you can regenerate the other keys.

That is the basic idea behind a deterministic wallet.

## **4.3 Hierarchical Deterministic (HD) wallet**

Important 5-mark and 10-mark topic

### **Definition**

A **Hierarchical Deterministic (HD) wallet is a type of deterministic wallet in which keys are organised in a hierarchical tree structure, with master, parent, child and further descendant keys derived from a seed.**

Your teacher's PPT specifically mentions BIP32 and BIP44 in connection with HD wallets.

### **First understand the word hierarchical**

Hierarchical means organised in levels.

For example, consider a university:

**University**

School A

School B

Dept A1

Dept A2

Dept B1

Dept B2

The university is at the top, schools are below it, and departments are further down.

An HD wallet follows a similar hierarchy for keys.

### **How does an HD wallet work?**

**Step 1:** A random seed is generated.

**Step 2:** The seed is used to generate a master key.

**Step 3:** The master key is used to derive child keys.

**Step 4:** Child keys can derive further child keys.

**Step 5:** The wallet can organise keys into multiple branches.

**Seed**

**Master key**

Child key A

Child key B

Child A1

Child A2

Child B1

Child B2

### **Important characteristics**

1. Keys are derived from a common seed.
2. Keys are arranged in a tree-like hierarchy.
3. Parent keys can derive child keys.
4. The wallet can generate many addresses.
5. A backup of the seed can restore the derived wallet keys, provided the relevant derivation and recovery information is preserved.

### **What is BIP32?**

BIP32 defines the hierarchical deterministic wallet structure and key derivation mechanism.

### **What is BIP44?**

BIP44 defines a commonly used hierarchy for organising multiple cryptocurrency accounts and address branches within HD wallets.

### **Advantages**

- Easy backup and recovery.
- Better organisation of multiple keys.
- Can generate many addresses from one seed.
- Convenient for wallets managing multiple accounts.
- More manageable than independent key backups.

### **Disadvantages**

- The seed must be protected carefully.
- If the recovery seed is compromised, many or all derived funds may be at risk.
- The hierarchy and derivation paths can be confusing for beginners.

### **Real-life example**

Imagine a tree:

- Root = Master key.
- Main branches = Child keys.
- Smaller branches = Further child keys.
- Leaves = Individual keys used for addresses.

All these keys are derived through a structured hierarchy.

**Memory trick:** Seed → Master → Parent → Child → Address.

## **4.4 Brain wallet**

### **Definition**

A **brain wallet is a wallet in which a private key is derived from a password or passphrase memorised by the user.**

The idea is simple: instead of writing down a recovery secret, the user remembers a phrase and uses it to derive the key.

### **How does it work?**

1. The user chooses a passphrase.
2. A cryptographic process derives a private key from that passphrase.
3. The corresponding public key and address can be generated.
4. The user can regenerate the same key by using the same passphrase and derivation procedure.

### **Example**

Suppose Aman chooses a phrase:

`Aman loves blockchain technology`

A wallet could hash the phrase to derive a private key.

However, this is dangerous if the phrase is predictable.

### **Advantages**

1. The user does not need to carry a physical wallet.
2. The passphrase can be remembered.
3. The key can be regenerated from the same phrase.

### **Disadvantages**

1. Weak or common phrases can be guessed.
2. Attackers can use dictionary attacks.
3. Brute-force attacks can be used to search possible passwords.
4. If someone guesses the phrase, they may gain control of the associated Bitcoin.

Security warning

Brain wallets based on user-chosen phrases are unsafe in practice. Even a phrase that seems difficult to guess may be predictable compared with a properly generated random seed.

Your teacher's PPT and textbook both specifically mention password guessing and brute-force attacks as weaknesses.

**Remember:** Brain wallet = Remembered phrase → Derived private key.

# **Topic 5: Hot Wallets vs Cold Wallets**

This is a very important comparison question.

Before understanding it, remember:

- Hot and cold describe how keys are connected or exposed to online systems.
- They are not completely separate wallet-generation categories.

For example, an HD wallet can be hot or cold depending on how it is used.

## **5.1 What is a hot wallet?**

**A hot wallet is a Bitcoin wallet whose keys are managed in an internet-connected environment or are readily accessible to an online system for transactions.**

Examples:

- Many mobile wallets
- Online wallets
- Desktop wallets connected to the internet

### **Advantages**

1. Convenient for regular payments.
2. Easy access.
3. Suitable for frequent transactions.
4. Quick to send and receive Bitcoin.

### **Disadvantages**

1. More exposed to online threats.
2. Malware can create security risks.
3. Phishing attacks may compromise wallet access.
4. Device or account compromise can expose funds.

## **5.2 What is a cold wallet?**

**A cold wallet is a wallet arrangement in which private keys are kept offline and isolated from ordinary internet-connected systems.**

Examples:

- Properly configured hardware wallets
- Carefully generated and stored paper wallets
- Other offline key-storage arrangements

### **Advantages**

1. Reduces exposure to online attacks.
2. Suitable for longer-term storage.
3. Private keys can remain offline.
4. Reduces some risks associated with malware on everyday devices.

### **Disadvantages**

1. Less convenient for frequent transactions.
2. Requires careful physical storage.
3. Can be lost or damaged.
4. Transactions require an additional signing and broadcasting process.

## **5.3 Hot wallet vs cold wallet comparison**

| Basis                  | Hot wallet                             | Cold wallet                                   |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Internet connection    | Keys are used in an online environment | Keys are kept offline                         |
| Convenience            | High                                   | Lower                                         |
| Online attack exposure | Higher                                 | Lower                                         |
| Transaction speed      | Generally convenient                   | May require additional steps                  |
| Usage                  | Regular payments                       | Long-term storage                             |
| Examples               | Online and many mobile wallets         | Hardware and paper wallets when properly used |

# **Topic 6: Wallet Backup and Recovery**

Now suppose Aman has 5 BTC in his wallet.

One day, his phone stops working.

Does he lose his Bitcoin?

Not necessarily.

If Aman has properly backed up his wallet recovery information, he may be able to restore access using another compatible wallet.

This is why backup and recovery are extremely important.

## **6.1 What is wallet backup?**

**Wallet backup is the process of safely creating and preserving a copy of the information required to recover a Bitcoin wallet and its keys.**

Your teacher's PPT specifically mentions backing up cryptographic keys as one of the functions of wallet software.

### **Why is backup necessary?**

1. A mobile phone may be lost.
2. A computer may be damaged.
3. A wallet application may be deleted.
4. A storage device may fail.
5. A user may accidentally lose access to the wallet.

A backup helps recover wallet access in such situations.

## **6.2 What is wallet recovery?**

**Wallet recovery is the process of restoring access to a Bitcoin wallet using previously saved recovery information.**

For a modern deterministic wallet, this commonly involves restoring from the seed or recovery phrase and using the appropriate wallet derivation settings.

### **Example**

Suppose Aman uses an HD wallet.

He has a recovery phrase stored safely.

His mobile phone is damaged.

He installs a compatible wallet on a new phone and restores it using the recovery phrase and the correct wallet settings.

The wallet can regenerate the associated keys and addresses.

**Original wallet**

Contains derived keys

**Create and securely store recovery backup**

**Phone is lost or damaged**

**Install compatible wallet on new device**

**Restore using recovery information**

# **Topic 7: Seed Phrase and Mnemonic Code**

Very important concept

## **7.1 What is a seed?**

A **seed is a randomly generated value used by a deterministic wallet to derive cryptographic keys.**

Instead of backing up every private key separately, the wallet can use the seed to regenerate the keys.

## **7.2 What is a mnemonic phrase?**

A **mnemonic phrase is a sequence of human-readable words used to represent recovery information for a wallet.**

For example, a wallet might provide a 12-word or 24-word recovery phrase.

Your textbook explains that BIP39 defines mnemonic code words used in deterministic wallet recovery.

## **7.3 How does a mnemonic phrase work?**

1. The wallet generates random entropy.
2. A checksum is added according to the mnemonic-generation procedure.
3. The result is divided into groups of bits.
4. Each group maps to a word in the defined word list.
5. The resulting words form a mnemonic phrase.
6. The phrase can be converted into seed material using the defined derivation procedure.
7. The seed is used to derive wallet keys.

The important relationship is:

\\[ \text{Mnemonic phrase} \rightarrow \text{Seed} \rightarrow \text{Wallet keys} \\]

## **7.4 What is BIP39?**

**BIP39 is a Bitcoin Improvement Proposal that defines a method for representing wallet recovery information as mnemonic words and deriving seed material from those words.**

### **Advantages of mnemonic phrases**

1. Easier to write down than a long private key.
2. Easier to back up.
3. Can restore keys in compatible wallets.
4. Supports deterministic wallet recovery.

### **Disadvantages and risks**

1. Anyone who obtains the phrase may gain access to the wallet.
2. Losing the phrase can make recovery difficult or impossible if no other backup exists.
3. Typing errors or missing words can prevent recovery.
4. Users must use compatible wallet settings and derivation paths to recover the expected addresses.

## **7.5 Seed phrase vs private key**

This is a common source of confusion.

| Seed phrase                                  | Private key                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Recovery information represented by words    | A secret cryptographic number                     |
| Can derive multiple wallet keys              | Controls spending under its associated conditions |
| Commonly used for wallet restoration         | Used to create digital signatures                 |
| Can provide access to a whole derived wallet | Usually relates to a particular key               |

**Very important:** A recovery phrase is often more powerful than a single private key because it can regenerate many keys in a wallet.

Never share your recovery phrase with anyone. A genuine wallet provider should not need you to disclose it to customer support.

# **Topic 8: Wallet Security**

A Bitcoin wallet is only as safe as the way its keys and recovery information are protected.

Let's understand the main security practices.

## **8.1 Protect the private key**

The private key must remain secret.

If an attacker obtains the key, the attacker may be able to authorise spending of the associated Bitcoin.

## **8.2 Protect the recovery phrase**

A seed or recovery phrase can restore the wallet's keys.

It should be stored securely and never shared through messages, emails or untrusted websites.

## **8.3 Use a strong device password**

Protect the computer or mobile device on which the wallet operates.

## **8.4 Use two-factor authentication where available**

For online wallet accounts, two-factor authentication can provide an additional security layer.

However, 2FA does not replace protecting private keys or recovery phrases.

## **8.5 Beware of phishing attacks**

Phishing is an attack in which someone tricks a user into revealing sensitive information through fake websites, messages or applications.

Example:

Aman receives an email saying:

"Your Bitcoin wallet is blocked. Click here and enter your recovery phrase."

The website is fake and designed to steal his recovery phrase.

## **8.6 Keep backups in secure locations**

A recovery backup should be protected against:

- Theft
- Fire
- Water damage
- Accidental deletion
- Unauthorised access

## **8.7 Verify transaction details**

Before signing a transaction, verify:

- Recipient address
- Amount
- Network
- Transaction fee

This is especially important when using hardware wallets.

## **8.8 Use genuine wallet software and devices**

Download wallet applications from trusted sources and purchase hardware wallets through trusted channels.

Avoid unknown applications or devices that could have been modified to steal keys.

## **C. Exam-ready 10-mark answer: Explain different types of Bitcoin wallets**

**Introduction:**

A Bitcoin wallet is a software application or physical device used to generate, store and manage cryptographic keys. It allows users to send, receive and manage Bitcoin transactions. A wallet does not actually store bitcoins; it manages the keys required to control the unspent transaction outputs recorded on the blockchain.

**Types of Bitcoin wallets:**

**1. Non-deterministic wallet:** It generates private keys independently and randomly. Each key requires appropriate backup. It is also called a Just a Bunch of Keys (JBOK) wallet.

**2. Deterministic wallet:** It generates multiple private keys from a single random seed using cryptographic functions. The seed can be used to regenerate the keys.

**3. Hierarchical Deterministic wallet:** It organises keys in a tree structure consisting of master, parent and child keys. It is defined through BIP32 and BIP44 and supports convenient key management.

**4. Brain wallet:** It derives private keys from a password or passphrase remembered by the user. It is vulnerable to password guessing and brute-force attacks.

**5. Paper wallet:** It stores private-key information on paper, sometimes in QR-code format. It requires protection against theft, loss and physical damage.

**6. Hardware wallet:** It uses a dedicated physical device to protect private keys and authorise transactions. Examples include Ledger and Trezor.

**7. Online wallet:** It is provided through an internet-based service and allows users to manage Bitcoin transactions through a web interface. It requires consideration of the service provider's security.

**8. Mobile wallet:** It is installed on a smartphone and allows users to send and receive Bitcoin, often using QR-code scanning.

**Conclusion:**

Bitcoin wallets provide secure key management and convenient access to Bitcoin transactions. The choice of wallet depends on security, convenience, backup requirements and user needs.
