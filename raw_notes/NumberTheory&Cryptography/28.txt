# 📘 MODULE 3: The RSA Cryptosystem

### 1. The Mathematical Foundation (The Trapdoor)
RSA was invented by Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman in 1977. It relies on the fact that:
*   **Easy:** Multiplying two large primes \(p\) and \(q\) to get \(n = p \times q\).
*   **Hard:** Factoring \(n\) back into \(p\) and \(q\) (if \(p\) and \(q\) are 200 digits each, it takes billions of years).

Because of this, the public key can include \(n\), but only the person who knows the factors \(p\) and \(q\) can find the private decryption key.

---

### 2. RSA Key Generation (Step-by-Step)

To create a public and private key, follow these exact steps:
1.  **Choose two distinct large primes**, \(p\) and \(q\).
2.  **Compute the modulus:** \(n = p \times q\).
    *(Note: The public key will be \((n, e)\)).*
3.  **Compute Euler’s Totient:** \(\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)\).
4.  **Choose a public exponent \(e\):** Choose an integer \(e\) such that \(1 < e < \phi(n)\) and **\(\gcd(e, \phi(n)) = 1\)**. (This means \(e\) and \(\phi(n)\) share no common factors).
5.  **Compute the private exponent \(d\):** Find the modular inverse of \(e\) modulo \(\phi(n)\). 
    This means solving: **\(e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}\)**. (You use the Extended Euclidean Algorithm from Module 1 for this).
    *(Note: The private key is \(d\) or \((n, d)\)).*

**Public Key:** \((n, e)\)
**Private Key:** \(d\) (or \((n, d)\))

---

### 3. RSA Encryption

To encrypt a message, we convert the text to numbers (A=00, B=01, ..., Z=25).
We split the numbers into blocks \(M\) such that \(M < n\).
The encryption formula is:
\[
C = M^e \bmod n
\]
*(You use Fast Modular Exponentiation to calculate this).*

---

### 4. RSA Decryption

To decrypt the ciphertext \(C\), we use the private key \(d\):
\[
M = C^d \bmod n
\]
*(Again, use Fast Modular Exponentiation).*

### 📝 Solved Exam Questions

Here is how RSA is tested in exams.

**Question 1 (Key Generation - 5 Marks):**
Let \(p = 3\) and \(q = 11\). Choose \(e = 7\). Find the public key, private key, and \(\phi(n)\).
**Solution:**
1.  \(n = p \times q = 3 \times 11 = 33\).
2.  \(\phi(n) = (3-1)(11-1) = 2 \times 10 = 20\).
3.  Check \(\gcd(7, 20) = 1\). (Yes, 7 and 20 share no factors).
4.  **Public Key:** \((n, e) = (33, 7)\).
5.  Find \(d\) such that \(7d \equiv 1 \pmod{20}\).
    Test values: \(7(3) = 21 \equiv 1 \pmod{20}\). So, \(d = 3\).
    **Private Key:** \(d = 3\).

**Question 2 (Full RSA Calculation - 10 Marks):**
*This is a classic textbook question (similar to Example 8 & 9 on Pages 7-8).*
Given \(p = 43\), \(q = 59\), and \(e = 13\).
(a) Encrypt the message "STOP".
(b) Decrypt the ciphertext "0981 0461".
**Solution:**
1.  **Setup:**
    \(n = 43 \times 59 = 2537\).
    \(\phi(n) = (42)(58) = 2436\).
    Public Key = \((2537, 13)\).
2.  **Encrypt "STOP":**
    Convert letters to numbers (A=00...Z=25): S=18, T=19, O=14, P=15.
    Message string: 1819 1415.
    Since \(n = 2537\), we use blocks of 4 digits (because \(2525 < 2537 < 252525\)).
    Block 1: \(M_1 = 1819\). \(C_1 = 1819^{13} \bmod 2537 = 2081\).
    Block 2: \(M_2 = 1415\). \(C_2 = 1415^{13} \bmod 2537 = 2182\).
    **Ciphertext:** 2081 2182.
3.  **Decrypt "0981 0461":**
    First, we need the private key \(d\). We solve \(13d \equiv 1 \pmod{2436}\).
    Using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm (from Module 1), we find \(d = 937\).
    *(You can verify: \(13 \times 937 = 12181\). \(12181 - 5 \times 2436 = 12181 - 12180 = 1\).)*
    Now, decrypt the blocks using \(M = C^{937} \bmod 2537\):
    Block 1: \(C_1 = 981\). \(M_1 = 981^{937} \bmod 2537 = 704\).
    Block 2: \(C_2 = 461\). \(M_2 = 461^{937} \bmod 2537 = 1115\).
    Message numbers: 0704 1115.
    Convert to letters (07=H, 04=E, 11=L, 15=P).
    **Plaintext:** "HELP".

*(Note for Exams: In a 10-mark question, you will not be expected to do \(981^{937}\) by hand. The question will either give you the answer, or the numbers will be small enough to use tricks. The crucial part they grade is your **setup** and your **decryption formula**.)*

**Question 3 (Theoretical/Proof - 5 Marks):**
Why does RSA Decryption work? Prove that \((M^e)^d \equiv M \pmod n\).
**Solution:**
By definition, \(e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}\). 
This means there exists an integer \(k\) such that \(ed = 1 + k(p-1)(q-1)\).
Therefore:
\(C^d \equiv (M^e)^d = M^{ed} = M^{1 + k(p-1)(q-1)} = M \cdot (M^{p-1})^{k(q-1)} \pmod n\).
By Fermat's Little Theorem (since \(p\) is prime), \(M^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p\).
So, modulo \(p\), \(C^d \equiv M \cdot 1 = M \pmod p\).
Similarly, modulo \(q\), \(C^d \equiv M \pmod q\).
By the **Chinese Remainder Theorem**, since \(C^d \equiv M \pmod p\) and \(C^d \equiv M \pmod q\), it must be true that \(C^d \equiv M \pmod{pq}\), which is \(n\).
Thus, \(M = C^d \bmod n\). **Q.E.D.**

---

### 5. Why is RSA Secure? (For MCQs)
An attacker knows the public key \((n, e)\). To find the private key \(d\), the attacker needs \(\phi(n)\). To find \(\phi(n)\), the attacker must know \(p\) and \(q\). To find \(p\) and \(q\), the attacker must **factor \(n\)**. 
Since factoring large \(n\) is computationally infeasible, RSA is secure.
