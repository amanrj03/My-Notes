## Part A — High-yield formula sheet

### Unit I: Number Theory-I

1\. Division Algorithm

\\[ a=dq+r,\qquad 0\le r\<d \\]

2\. Congruence

\\[ a\equiv b\pmod m\iff m\mid(a-b) \\]

3\. Cancellation

From \\(ac\equiv bc\pmod m\\), conclude

\\[ a\equiv b\pmod{\frac{m}{\gcd(c,m)}}. \\]

If \\(\gcd(c,m)=1\\), cancellation preserves modulus \\(m\\).

4\. Base-\\(b\\) representation

\\[ n=a_kb^k+\cdots+a_1b+a_0,\quad 0\le a_i\<b \\]

5\. Operation counts

Binary addition takes \\(O(n)\\) bit operations; conventional binary multiplication takes \\(O(n^2)\\).

### Unit II: Number Theory-II

6\. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every integer greater than \\(1\\) has a unique prime factorization, apart from the order of factors.

7\. Trial division

If \\(n\\) is composite, it has a prime divisor at most \\(\sqrt n\\).

8\. Prime Number Theorem

\\[ \pi(x)\sim\frac{x}{\ln x} \\]

9\. GCD and LCM

\\[ \gcd(a,b)\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=|ab| \\]

for integers \\(a,b\\) not both zero, using positive GCD and LCM.

10\. Euclidean algorithm and Bézout

\\[ \gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,a\bmod b) \\]

\\[ \gcd(a,b)=sa+tb \\]

for some integers \\(s,t\\).

### Unit III: Number Theory-III

11\. Linear congruence

\\(ax\equiv b\pmod m\\) is solvable exactly when \\(\gcd(a,m)\mid b\\). If solvable, it has exactly \\(\gcd(a,m)\\) distinct solution classes modulo \\(m\\).

12\. Chinese Remainder Theorem

Pairwise relatively prime moduli give a unique solution class modulo their product.

13\. Fermat's Little Theorem

\\[ a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p \\]

if \\(p\\) is prime and \\(p\nmid a\\).

14\. Modular inverse

\\[ a^{-1}\equiv a^{p-2}\pmod p \\]

when \\(p\\) is prime and \\(p\nmid a\\).

15\. Large-integer arithmetic

Residues can be added and multiplied componentwise modulo pairwise coprime moduli. Exact reconstruction requires a sufficiently large product of moduli.

## Part C — Final preparation strategy

Before your exam, make sure you can do these without notes:

- Find quotients and nonnegative remainders, including for negative integers.
- Simplify modular expressions and recognize when cancellation is valid.
- Convert between decimal, binary, octal and hexadecimal.
- Factor integers into primes and apply the square-root bound.
- Use the Euclidean algorithm and express a GCD as a linear combination.
- Solve linear congruences and systems using CRT.
- Reduce large exponents with Fermat's Little Theorem.
- Recognize pseudoprimes and understand the limitations of the theorem.
- Explain the role of congruences in pseudorandom sequences, memory assignment and check digits.

The most important exam habit: check theorem conditions before applying a formula. Many difficult-looking MCQs become straightforward once you identify whether the modulus is prime, whether the numbers are relatively prime, and whether the question asks for a remainder or a full solution class.
