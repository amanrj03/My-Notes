Topic 1: Introduction to Bitcoin Payments

1.1 What are Bitcoin payments?

First, understand a normal payment.

Suppose you go to a shop and buy a mobile phone worth ₹20,000.

You can pay using:

  • Cash
  • Debit card
  • Credit card
  • UPI

Now imagine the shop also accepts Bitcoin.

Instead of paying ₹20,000 through UPI or a card, you can transfer the required amount of Bitcoin to the shop's Bitcoin address.

This is called a Bitcoin payment.

Definition

Bitcoin payment is the transfer of Bitcoin from one user to another as consideration for goods or services, using a transaction recorded on the Bitcoin blockchain.

For example:

  • Aman purchases a laptop from a shop.
  • The shop accepts Bitcoin.
  • The shop provides a Bitcoin payment address.
  • Aman uses his Bitcoin wallet to send the required amount.
  • The transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network.
  • The transaction can then be confirmed through mining and block inclusion.

1.2 Why are businesses accepting Bitcoin?

Your textbook explains that Bitcoin is not recognised as legal currency in many jurisdictions, but it is increasingly accepted as a payment method by online merchants and e-commerce websites.

Businesses may accept Bitcoin because:

  1. It provides another payment option for customers.
  2. It allows payments through a decentralised network.
  3. It can support international payments.
  4. It provides an alternative to conventional payment systems.
  5. It can attract customers who prefer cryptocurrency payments.

Important distinction: Accepting Bitcoin as a payment method does not necessarily mean a country recognises Bitcoin as legal tender.

Topic 2: How Does a Bitcoin Payment Work?

Let's understand the complete payment process using a simple example.

Suppose Aman purchases a T-shirt worth ₹2,000 from a shop that accepts Bitcoin.

Step-by-step working

Step 1: Shop calculates the price

The shop calculates the price in its normal currency, such as Indian Rupees.

Step 2: Shop provides payment details

The shop displays a Bitcoin address, QR code or payment link.

Step 3: Customer opens the wallet

Aman opens his Bitcoin wallet on his mobile phone.

Step 4: Customer enters payment details

He scans the QR code or enters the Bitcoin address and amount.

Step 5: Wallet creates the transaction

The wallet selects suitable UTXOs and creates the transaction.

Step 6: Transaction is digitally signed

The wallet uses the relevant private key to authorise the transaction.

Step 7: Transaction is broadcast

The signed transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network.

Step 8: Transaction is confirmed

Miners may include the transaction in a block. Once included in an accepted block, it receives its first confirmation.

Step 9: Merchant receives payment

The merchant monitors the payment and updates the order status according to its confirmation policy.

Miners validate signatures and include the transaction in a newly mined PoW block. 1st confirmation achieved.

Merchant node detects confirmation, prints final receipt, and releases goods. Irreversible finality increases with each block.

Important point: Is a transaction confirmed immediately?

No.

Broadcasting a transaction and confirming a transaction are two different things.

  • Broadcast: The transaction is sent to the Bitcoin network.
  • Confirmation: The transaction is included in a block accepted by the network.

A merchant may display a payment as detected before it is confirmed, but the merchant must decide how many confirmations are required before treating the payment as sufficiently final.

For higher-value transactions, merchants may wait for additional confirmations.

Topic 3: Bitcoin Payments Using QR Codes

3.1 What is a QR code?

QR stands for Quick Response.

A QR code is a two-dimensional barcode that stores information that can be read using a camera or QR-code scanner.

In Bitcoin payments, the QR code can contain the merchant's Bitcoin payment URI.

This allows customers to scan the code instead of manually typing a long Bitcoin address.

3.2 How does QR-based Bitcoin payment work?

Suppose Aman visits a coffee shop.

The bill is ₹300.

The shop displays a QR code on its payment terminal.

The process is:

  1. The shop generates payment information.
  2. The payment information is encoded into a QR code.
  3. Aman opens his Bitcoin wallet.
  4. He scans the QR code.
  5. His wallet reads the Bitcoin address and any supplied payment details.
  6. Aman checks the payment information.
  7. He confirms the transaction.
  8. The wallet signs and broadcasts the transaction.

3.3 Advantages of QR-code payments

  1. No need to manually type long Bitcoin addresses.
  2. Reduces typing mistakes.
  3. Makes mobile payments convenient.
  4. Faster checkout experience.
  5. Can encode the payment amount and other optional information.

3.4 Limitation

A QR code does not itself transfer Bitcoin.

It only provides payment information to the wallet.

The actual Bitcoin transfer happens when the wallet creates, signs and broadcasts the transaction.

Remember: QR code = Payment information, not the payment itself.

Topic 4: Bitcoin URI and BIP21

Important 5-mark question

This is one of the most important concepts in Bitcoin payments.

4.1 First, what is a URI?

URI stands for Uniform Resource Identifier.

A URI is a string that identifies a resource or provides information needed to access or use it.

For example:

https://www.example.com

This is a web URI.

Similarly, Bitcoin defines its own payment URI format.

4.2 What is a Bitcoin URI?

A Bitcoin URI is a specially formatted string that contains Bitcoin payment information, such as the recipient's Bitcoin address, amount and optional parameters.

It allows a user to initiate a Bitcoin payment by clicking a link or scanning a QR code.

Your textbook explains that Bitcoin URIs are defined in BIP21 and are supported by nearly all Bitcoin wallets.

4.3 What is BIP21?

BIP21 is a Bitcoin Improvement Proposal that defines a standard URI scheme for making Bitcoin payments.

Its purpose is to make Bitcoin payments easier by allowing payment information to be represented in a standard format.

Example of a Bitcoin URI

bitcoin:1BoatSLRHtKNngkdXEeobR76b53LETtpyT?amount=0.01

Let's break it down.

Part Meaning
bitcoin: Identifies the Bitcoin URI scheme
1Boat... Recipient's Bitcoin address
? Starts the optional parameters
amount=0.01 Requests a payment of 0.01 BTC

This means that the URI requests a payment of 0.01 BTC to the specified Bitcoin address.

4.4 Important parameters in Bitcoin URI

A Bitcoin URI can contain different optional parameters.

Parameter Purpose
address Recipient address is supplied directly after bitcoin:
amount Specifies the payment amount in BTC
label Provides a human-readable label
message Provides a payment message
r Refers to a payment-request resource used by the payment protocol

Example:

bitcoin:1BoatSLRHtKNngkdXEeobR76b53LETtpyT?amount=0.01&label=CoffeeShop&message=Order123

Here:

  • Address identifies the recipient.
  • Amount specifies 0.01 BTC.
  • Label displays CoffeeShop.
  • Message contains Order123.

Topic 7: Bitcoin Payment Protocol — BIP70

Very important 10-mark question

Now we are moving to one of the most important technical topics in Bitcoin payments.

Before understanding BIP70, let's understand the problem it tries to address.

7.1 What is the problem with ordinary Bitcoin payments?

Suppose Aman wants to purchase a mobile phone from an online store.

The store displays a Bitcoin address.

Aman copies the address and sends Bitcoin.

But imagine an attacker changes the displayed address and replaces it with their own Bitcoin address.

Aman may believe he is paying the shop, but actually he is sending Bitcoin to the attacker.

This is an example of a Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attack.

Another problem is that ordinary payment information does not necessarily provide a standardised, authenticated communication process between the merchant and customer.

Bitcoin's Payment Protocol was proposed to address such issues.

7.2 What is BIP70?

BIP70 is the Bitcoin Payment Protocol, designed to provide secure communication between a merchant and a customer during a Bitcoin payment.

Your teacher's PPT and textbook highlight these features:

  1. Secure communication between merchant and customer.
  2. Uses X.509 certificates for authentication.
  3. Runs over HTTP and HTTPS.
  4. Provides defence against Man-in-the-Middle attacks.
  5. Supports secure proof of payment.

What is X.509?

X.509 is a standard format for digital certificates.

A digital certificate can be used to associate a public key with an identity, such as a merchant's identity.

In the BIP70 design, certificates are used to authenticate the merchant's payment request.

What is the purpose of BIP70?

Imagine that Aman is paying an online shop.

He should be able to verify that the payment request is genuinely associated with the intended merchant rather than an attacker who has modified the payment details.

BIP70 introduces a structured protocol for this communication.

7.3 Three important messages in BIP70

Must remember for exam

BIP70 has three important messages:

  1. PaymentRequest
  2. Payment
  3. PaymentACK

Let's understand them one by one.

1. PaymentRequest

PaymentRequest is a message sent by the merchant to the customer containing the payment details and merchant authentication information.

It can include:

  • Payment amount
  • Merchant payment details
  • Payment destination information
  • Expiration information
  • Merchant certificate information

Example:

Aman wants to purchase a laptop worth ₹50,000.

The merchant sends a PaymentRequest containing the payment information and authentication details.

Aman's wallet can verify the merchant's certificate before presenting the payment request for approval.

2. Payment

Payment is a message sent by the customer's wallet to the merchant after the customer makes the payment.

It contains information about the transaction.

It may also include transaction details and a refund-related output.

Example:

Aman verifies the payment request and authorises the transaction.

His wallet sends the payment information to the merchant.

3. PaymentACK

PaymentACK is an acknowledgement message sent by the merchant to the customer after receiving the Payment message.

It acts as an acknowledgement of the payment submission and can include a memo or confirmation information.

Example:

After receiving Aman's payment message, the merchant sends PaymentACK to acknowledge receipt of the payment information.

Easy memory trick

  • PaymentRequest = Merchant asks for payment.
  • Payment = Customer sends payment information.
  • PaymentACK = Merchant acknowledges payment.

Topic 8: Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack in Bitcoin Payments

8.1 What is a Man-in-the-Middle attack?

A Man-in-the-Middle attack is a security attack in which an attacker secretly intercepts or modifies communication between two parties without their knowledge.

Let's understand it through a simple example.

Aman wants to pay ₹5,000 to an online shop.

The shop provides a Bitcoin payment address.

An attacker intercepts the communication and replaces the shop's Bitcoin address with the attacker's address.

Aman thinks he is paying the shop, but he is actually sending Bitcoin to the attacker.

This is a Man-in-the-Middle attack.

8.3 How does BIP70 help prevent this attack?

MITM Attack Prevention: Plain Address vs. X.509 Signed BIP70

Cryptographic public key infrastructure guarantees payment recipient identity
❌ Without BIP70 (Raw Bitcoin Address)
• Attacker intercepts insecure HTTP link or tampers with clipboard/DNS.
• Attacker swaps merchant address with attacker's address.
• Customer wallet has no way to verify identity → Funds stolen!
✅ With BIP70 (X.509 PKI Authenticated)
• PaymentRequest is signed with merchant's trusted SSL/TLS Certificate.
• If attacker alters address, cryptographic signature check fails.
• Wallet displays: 🔒 Verified Merchant Name → 100% Protected!

BIP70 uses X.509 certificates to authenticate the merchant's payment request.

The basic idea is:

  1. Merchant sends a PaymentRequest.
  2. The request contains certificate information.
  3. The customer's wallet verifies the merchant's certificate.
  4. The wallet can display the authenticated merchant identity.
  5. The customer can check that the payment request belongs to the intended merchant.

This makes it harder for an attacker to silently replace the merchant's payment details with their own while still appearing to be the authenticated merchant.

Important exam point

BIP70 was designed to provide authentication and defence against MITM attacks. It does not mean that every possible attack against a user's device, wallet or payment process is prevented.

Topic 9: BIP71 and BIP72

These two proposals are related to BIP70.

Your textbook specifically mentions both.

9.1 What is BIP71?

BIP71 defines MIME types for the Bitcoin Payment Protocol.

First understand MIME.

MIME stands for Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions.

MIME types help systems identify the format and type of information being transmitted.

In the Bitcoin Payment Protocol, BIP71 standardises the MIME types used for payment-protocol messages.

In simple words:

BIP71 helps different software systems identify and handle Bitcoin payment-protocol data consistently.

9.2 What is BIP72?

BIP72 defines URI extensions that allow Bitcoin URIs to reference the Bitcoin Payment Protocol.

Remember BIP21?

BIP21 defines a standard Bitcoin URI.

BIP72 extends the URI mechanism to support BIP70 payment requests.

This allows a payment URI to refer to a payment-request resource rather than simply containing an ordinary payment address and amount.

9.3 Difference between BIP21, BIP70, BIP71 and BIP72

Bitcoin Payment Standards Ecosystem Stack (BIP21 → BIP70/71/72)

Layered evolution from simple static URIs to authenticated enterprise payment protocols
Layer 3: BIP70 — Secure Payment Protocol PKI & Security
Defines Protobuf messages (PaymentRequest, Payment, PaymentACK) secured by X.509 digital certificates to eliminate MITM address spoofing and provide cryptographic payment receipts.
Layer 2B: BIP71 — Payment Protocol MIME Types Content Framing
Standardises HTTP Content-Types (e.g. application/bitcoin-paymentrequest) so operating systems and web browsers cleanly dispatch payment data to wallet handlers.
Layer 2A: BIP72 — Payment Protocol URI Extensions Link Extension
Extends BIP21 with the r=https://... parameter, enabling a Bitcoin URI to dynamically fetch an authenticated BIP70 PaymentRequest via HTTPS.
Layer 1: BIP21 — Base Bitcoin Payment URI Base Format
Foundational URI scheme (bitcoin:<address>?amount=...) used across all mobile QR code scanning and direct link payments.

Topic 10: Advantages and Limitations of Bitcoin Payments

Advantages

1. Decentralised payment system

Bitcoin payments are processed through a decentralised network rather than depending on one central payment authority.

2. Global accessibility

Bitcoin can be used for cross-border payments where the relevant services and regulations permit.

3. Transparency

Transactions are recorded on the public blockchain.

4. No traditional card-network requirement

A Bitcoin payment does not require the conventional card-network infrastructure used for debit and credit card payments.

5. Merchant integration

Businesses can use QR codes, POS terminals and payment processors to accept Bitcoin.

6. Cryptographic security

Private-key-based digital signatures help authorise transactions.

Limitations

1. Price volatility

Bitcoin's market price can change significantly, affecting the value received by merchants.

2. Confirmation time

A transaction is not necessarily confirmed immediately after being broadcast.

3. Irreversible payments

Once a transaction is confirmed, reversing it is generally not possible through a normal card-style chargeback mechanism.

4. Technical complexity

Customers and businesses need to understand wallets, addresses, keys and transaction fees.

5. Regulatory differences

Bitcoin's legal and regulatory treatment differs across jurisdictions.

6. Security risks

Phishing, malware, stolen private keys and fraudulent payment requests can result in financial loss.

C. Exam-ready 10-mark answer: Explain BIP70 Payment Protocol

Introduction:

BIP70 is the Bitcoin Payment Protocol designed to provide secure communication between merchants and customers during Bitcoin payments. It uses X.509 digital certificates for authentication and operates over HTTP and HTTPS.

Objectives of BIP70:

  1. Provide secure communication between merchant and customer.
  2. Authenticate the merchant's payment request.
  3. Protect against Man-in-the-Middle attacks.
  4. Provide a structured payment process.
  5. Support secure proof of payment.

Three important messages:

1. PaymentRequest: It is sent by the merchant to the customer and contains payment details and certificate information.

2. Payment: It is sent by the customer's wallet to the merchant after the customer authorises payment. It contains transaction information.

3. PaymentACK: It is sent by the merchant to acknowledge receipt of the Payment message.

Working:

  1. Merchant creates a PaymentRequest.
  2. Customer's wallet receives the request.
  3. Wallet verifies the merchant's certificate.
  4. Customer checks and approves payment.
  5. Wallet creates and signs the Bitcoin transaction.
  6. Wallet sends a Payment message to the merchant.
  7. Merchant sends PaymentACK.
  8. The transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin network and can be confirmed through block inclusion.

Advantages:

  • Provides merchant authentication.
  • Helps prevent MITM attacks.
  • Provides structured payment communication.
  • Supports proof-of-payment information.
  • Improves security in merchant-customer communication.

Conclusion:

BIP70 provides a standardised and authenticated communication mechanism for Bitcoin payments, helping merchants and customers exchange payment information securely.
